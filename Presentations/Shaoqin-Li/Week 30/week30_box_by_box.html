Three different clips with three different cameras cannot isolate the effect of the camera.

What it means

Three clips differ in motion, scene and camera at once.

Limit 3: no ground truth

I have no true human pose for these stock clips, so robot tracking error is not a measure of how well GVHMR reconstructed the person.

What it means

A low robot tracking error would not prove GVHMR got the human right; a high one would not prove it got it wrong.

The approach I propose
Measure first, then tune the simulator, then check on motions it has not seen

What it means

The order matters: measure, tune, check on new motions, then use.

Step 1

What it means

Existing logs first. New recordings wait for the gantry.

Step 2

2. Fit
the delay, how fast each motor responds, and its limits and friction

What it means

Fit delay, motor response, limits and friction as simple models.

Step 3

3. Check
predict a motion I did not use for fitting and compare it with the recording

What it means

Step 4

4. Use
put the measured ranges into the training randomization

Measured ranges replace my guessed ranges in training.

Caveats

A learned actuator model is a later option, if the simple fit still leaves a systematic gap. What it means

Figure credit

Figure 2 of the paper (Lau et al.).

Authors' figure.

Card a

a. Build a skill graph
Each motion frame is a point. Similar frames of different skills get a link, with extra buffer frames where the gap is large.

What it means

Think of a route map. Each frame is a stop. Links join stops that look alike in different skills. Buffer frames fill a long gap with extra stops.

Card b

b. Train one tracker
The policy practices the linked transitions in simulation, with a reward that guides it through the buffer frames.

What it means

The policy sees the links during training, so switching is something it practised.

From the robot's current state, the scheduler finds the path to the commanded skill. The hardware runs the simpler nearest-neighbor variant.

What it means

The paper offers two planners. A full graph search and a nearest-neighbor lookup. The hardware demonstration used nearest-neighbor, so do not say the robot ran the full search.

Title

Why fluent is harder than it sounds
Three tracked clips are not a game

What it means

The three Part 1 clips each track one fixed motion. A game needs more.

Risk 1: steering

A policy that tracks one walking clip does not walk in an arbitrary direction or speed. The game needs a walk I can steer.

What it means

A tracking policy is closed-loop: it reacts to the state of the robot, but it follows one fixed reference motion. Steering needs a walk whose direction and speed come from a command.

Risk 2: switching

Switching needs the end of one skill to match the start of the next: matching speed, heading, and a stable posture.

What it means

Pose, velocity, foot contact and heading at the end of one skill must match what the next skill expects, so the next policy takes over without a reset to its own starting pose.

Risk 3: landing

Jumping adds a landing, so the switcher needs a recovery path when a landing goes wrong.

What it means

Landing changes the robot from flight to ground contact, and locomotion has to resume from the landed state. Blending between policies does not by itself establish balance, so a failed landing needs a recovery path.

Risk 4: grab

Grabbing adds hands and objects, which my pipeline does not handle yet. It comes last.

What it means

Grab is last because it needs hand, object and contact support that the pipeline does not have yet.
