WHAT THIS ADDS
  1. smplx is now the THIRD format run end to end (with bvh_xsens, bvh_lafan1).
     smplx_to_r1.json was written by hand months before the generator existed,
     and it is the file that uses the DIFFERENT arm ratio (0.617, not 0.578).
     It produces a clean, real-time, limit-respecting motion on a dataset it had
     never seen -- evidence the proportional scale rule is right rather than
     fitted.

  2. THE CONTACT GAP REPRODUCES ON A SECOND DATASET AND A SECOND FORMAT.
        LAFAN1 (13 categories, bvh)   R1  5.1%  vs G1 10.6%   (R1 = 48% of G1)
        OMOMO  (1 clip, smplx)        R1 78%    vs G1 99%     (R1 = 79% of G1)
     Different capture system, different skeleton, different motion type, same
     direction. So it is a property of the R1 setup, not a LAFAN1 artifact.

  3. A DETAIL THAT POINTS AT THE CAUSE. The R1 contacts the floor LESS often
     (78% vs 99%) yet penetrates DEEPER when it does (-2.33 vs -1.04 cm). Feet
     that were uniformly too high would contact less and penetrate less. Both
     at once means the foot target is landing in the wrong place with more
     spread, which is what a leg-length mismatch looks like.

     HYPOTHESIS (untested): the leg scale factor is the cause. The config uses
     0.818 for the legs, but the R1's measured segment ratios against the human
     are thigh 0.673 and shank 0.735 (measurements/config_and_links_output.txt).
     0.818 was tuned, not derived. A sweep of the leg scale against contact %,
     on clips from both datasets, would test it directly and is cheap.
